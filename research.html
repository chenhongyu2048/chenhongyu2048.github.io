LiquidCP: Decoupling Attention Tasks from Communication and Kernel Boundaries in Context Parallelism

Hongyu Chen, Yikang Ruan, Yuhai Long, Ziyu Huang, Yiyu Luo, Liukun Yu, Yangjie Zhou, Zihan Liu, Weihao Cui, Zijun Li, Anbang Wu, Yu Feng, Jing Yang, Jingwen Leng, Bingsheng He, Minyi Guo, Shixuan Sun.

arXiv preprint 2611.04401, November 2026.

A shared GPU execution engine for context-parallel attention in long-context training, prefill, and decoding.

arXiv ↗Paper PDF ↗
Abstract summary +

LiquidCP schedules context-parallel attention as tile-level transfer, compute, and reduction tasks. MegaRing balances attention work and token counts and groups ready KV regions for training and full prefill. MegaDCP chooses how to split historical KV for decoding and mixed batches by accounting for both attention and its dependent reductions. Against the fastest baseline for each configuration on eight H100 GPUs, MegaRing speeds up core attention by 1.23× in the forward pass and 1.18× in the backward pass; MegaDCP reaches 1.20× on decode-only batches and 1.35× on mixed batches. In vLLM with prefix caching, LiquidCP improves time to first token by 1.22× on average.

InfiniLoRA: Disaggregated Multi-LoRA Serving for Large Language Models

Hongyu Chen, Letian Ruan, Zilin Xu, Yuchen Li, Xinyu Chen, Bingsheng He, Jingwen Leng, Jieru Zhao, Shixuan Sun, Minyi Guo.

The 2026 ACM SIGOPS Annual Technical Conference.

Acceptance rate: 13.9% (135/973).

A shared LoRA server separates adapter execution from base-model inference and overlaps communication and computation across instances.

arXiv ↗Camera-ready PDF ↗
Abstract +

LoRA enables efficient customization of LLMs and is widely used in multi-tenant and multi-task serving. However, emerging model architectures such as MoE significantly increase LoRA memory cost, making existing coupled LoRA serving designs poorly scalable and prone to tail-latency inflation. We present InfiniLoRA, a disaggregated LoRA serving system that decouples LoRA execution from base-model inference. InfiniLoRA introduces a shared LoRA Server with parallelism-aware execution, SLO-driven provisioning, and critical-path optimizations, including GPU-initiated communication and hardware-specialized LoRA kernels. Experiments show that InfiniLoRA can achieve an average 3.05× increase in serviceable request rate under strict latency SLOs, and improve the percentage of LoRA adapters satisfying the SLO requirement by 54.0%.

Auto-Divide GNN: Accelerating GNN Training with Subgraph Division

Hongyu Chen*, Zhejiang Ran*, Keshi Ge, Zhiquan Lai, Jingfei Jiang, Dongsheng Li.

The 29th International European Conference on Parallel and Distributed Computing.

* Equal contribution.

Combining automatic subgraph division with GPU caching to accelerate sampling-based GNN training.

Publication ↗Paper PDF ↗
Abstract +

Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) have gained considerable attention in recent years for their exceptional performance on graph-structured data. Sampling-based GNN training is the most common method used for training GNNs on large-scale graphs, and it is often accelerated by caching feature data on the GPU. However, the emergence of more complex models and datasets with higher feature dimension requires more GPU memory for training, which limits the acceleration performance of GPU caching and can even result in out-of-memory errors. To release more GPU memory for the cache in a transparent way, we propose a subgraph division method, which trains several smaller micrographs instead of an entire subgraph at each training iteration. However, it is non-trivial to combine subgraph division with GPU caching due to the redundancy between micrographs. To tackle this challenge, we introduce an auto-profile method that searches for the best-performing subgraph division scheme based on training perception and probability analysis. Additionally, we develop an estimation-based caching strategy to lift the caching hitting rate against the diverse graph structures. These ideas are integrated to Auto-Divide GNN, a framework for accelerating sampling-based GNN training. The multi-GPU evaluations on three representative GNN models and five large graphs demonstrate that Auto-Divide GNN achieves significant speedups of up to 5.61× and 3.13× over two state-of-the-art GNN frameworks, DGL and PaGraph, respectively.
